## A certificate measures a rule, not an entire economy

Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment is both a policy framework and a commercial reality for many South African businesses. Its statutory architecture includes ownership, management control, skills development, enterprise and supplier development, and socio-economic development, subject to the applicable codes and sector rules \[1\]. Compliance can be necessary for procurement and market access. But a certificate does not, by itself, establish that an enterprise has created sustainable suppliers, transferable skills or lasting economic participation.

The B-BBEE Commission’s report on 2021 submissions identified R41.6 billion in skills-development spending and R26 billion in enterprise and supplier development spending in the reporting base \[2\]. Importantly, the Commission stated that its analysis did not establish beneficiary outcomes because the reporting rules did not require the necessary impact information. The sums relate to the analysed submissions, not a verified economy-wide assessment of results in every programme.

That gap between spending and outcomes is where strategy begins. An enterprise can spend the required amount on training that does not result in work. It can support a supplier whose equipment remains idle because there is no sustainable customer contract. It can record ownership interests without building influence, access to information or real participation in enterprise value. A beautiful scorecard can coexist with an unimpressive economy. It is possible to pass a test and still miss the purpose of the lesson.

This does not mean the policy objectives are unimportant or that measurement should be abandoned. It means a serious business should supplement statutory measures with outcome indicators that reveal whether economic capability is actually being built. Treat B-BBEE as a strategy question as well as a compliance question.

## From qualifying expenditure to measurable capability

The starting point is to identify where transformation can reinforce the operating model. A manufacturer can help a black-owned supplier achieve quality accreditation and then offer recurring purchase orders, subject to fair procurement practices. A financial services firm can combine technical training with monitored opportunities for progression into management. A construction group can support specialist subcontractors in health and safety, costing, project controls and access to equipment.

These choices require financial and commercial scrutiny. Enterprise-development grant funding is not equivalent to an order book. Supplier inclusion without payment discipline can damage the very businesses the programme intends to support. A large customer that negotiates long terms and pays late should not celebrate the annual supplier-development luncheon quite so loudly.

Track outcomes at three levels. First, participation: who received funding, training, mentorship or contracts? Second, capability: did participants achieve relevant accreditation, margins, governance systems and technical delivery standards? Third, durability: did they obtain repeat orders, retain workers, generate cash and access additional customers? Where lawful and appropriate, disaggregate outcomes to see whose access is improving and whose is not.

The applicable legal position must always be checked. The Department of Trade, Industry and Competition maintains the underlying Act, amended codes and sector-specific instruments \[1\]. Different entities may be subject to different measurement rules, and the legal framework can change. This article does not substitute for a current verification, legal or procurement opinion.

Better governance also protects integrity. Use independent verification, document beneficial interests, test related-party arrangements and ensure that the commercial substance matches the reported structure. Transformation loses public legitimacy when a compliance arrangement exists on paper but operational control and value are elsewhere.

## Compare beneficiaries before and after support

A credible programme begins with a baseline. Record beneficiary revenue, staffing, operating capacity, safety or quality accreditation, and access to customers before support begins. Follow up after the intervention, using consistent definitions and acknowledging external factors such as market conditions. A change in sales is not automatically caused by a training programme.

Separate mandatory scorecard evidence from evaluation evidence. Verification professionals need the relevant affidavits, certificates, records and calculations under the applicable code. Management needs to know whether participant firms can deliver independently and whether the programme actually expanded commercial opportunity. These are different questions that should inform each other.

Protect beneficiaries against dependency. An emerging supplier with one dominant customer may gain revenue but remain highly exposed. Encourage credible new customer access, quality systems, pricing discipline and working-capital management. Where a business cannot offer sustainable volumes, say so plainly. Sponsorship that ends with a ceremonial photograph and no route to market is a weak substitute for genuine enterprise development.

## A transformation operating plan

Start with a clear three-year target beyond the score. For suppliers, define the number of firms graduating to repeat commercial contracts, their defect rates, average payment days and independent customer diversification. For skills, track completion, demonstrable competence, placement and retention after six or twelve months. For ownership and management, monitor substantive participation, decision rights and meaningful development pathways.

Assign each programme a business owner and a beneficiary-outcomes owner. Set baseline measures before intervention. Commission credible outcome checks rather than measuring success only as the amount transferred. If ten participating suppliers receive support but only two become reliable commercial partners, diagnose the cause: insufficient demand, weak equipment, inappropriate specifications, delayed payment or poor supplier selection. Then redesign the programme.

An illustrative calculation makes the distinction concrete. Assume a development programme spends R2 million to support twenty businesses. The expenditure per participant is R100,000, but it says nothing about value generated. If twelve businesses subsequently secure repeat orders, report that separately, along with order value, margin and continuity. That is more useful than claiming twenty “success stories” before checking what happened next.

There is room for legitimate debate about the effectiveness, design and unintended consequences of empowerment policies. That debate deserves data and distinctions, not slogans. Businesses should comply with applicable law while also testing whether their interventions create capabilities that survive the end of the funded programme.

## Boardroom takeaway

Keep two dashboards: statutory B-BBEE performance and actual economic outcomes. The first supports compliance. The second tests whether transformation is working. If the numbers never meet, investigate why.
