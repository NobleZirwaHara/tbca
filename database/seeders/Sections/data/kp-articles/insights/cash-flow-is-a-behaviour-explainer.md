## Profit does not settle an overdue supplier account

An income statement can report a profitable quarter while the bank account is empty. A sale made today may not be collected for sixty days. Employees, rent and electricity rarely agree to wait that long. This is why liquidity depends on daily management choices rather than an attractive month-end report.

The Auditor-General’s 2024–25 municipal audit reported that South African municipalities took an average of 129 days to collect amounts owed, while 53% of municipalities failed to pay suppliers on time \[1\]. These are municipal-sector findings, not national averages for private businesses. They illustrate how collection and payment behaviour can affect an entire chain of organisations, including smaller suppliers with little buffer.

Consider a consulting firm delivering R1.2 million of work each month. If, purely for illustration, customers extend average collection time by fifteen days, approximately R600,000 of additional cash can become tied up in receivables: R1.2 million multiplied by 15/30. This simplified calculation assumes even monthly billings and ignores VAT, bad debts and changes in work in progress. It is a management illustration, not the cash-flow statement of an actual company.

The dangerous habit is to regard signed contracts as cash and unbilled work as income available to spend. Neither is true. Businesses must distinguish pipeline, contracted backlog, delivered work, invoiced amounts, undisputed receivables and cleared funds. Every transition has operational friction. A large order can actually increase financing pressure if the company must pay subcontractors and purchase stock before the customer settles.

## Seven behaviours that change the numbers

First, price correctly. Gross margin must cover the real cost of serving the customer, including delivery, warranty, admin and financing. Second, negotiate cash terms before work begins. Deposits, milestone billing, mobilisation advances and retention rules often matter more than a small change in headline price.

Third, invoice immediately and accurately. Incomplete purchase-order references and missing supporting documents can create avoidable disputes. Fourth, review receivables weekly by customer, age and reason for delay. A vague “follow up with finance” note is not a collection plan. Fifth, control procurement commitments against near-term cash capacity. Sixth, separate the tax and payroll obligations that cannot be funded with wishful thinking. Seventh, maintain a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast, updated with actual receipts and payment commitments.

A thirteen-week view is not a magic formula. It is a practical horizon for recognising a shortage before it becomes a crisis. Mark each major inflow by probability and evidence. Put large discretionary payments behind explicit approval gates. Forecast a downside case with late customer receipts or lower sales, and calculate the minimum liquidity buffer required to operate without breaching obligations.

Receivables financing can be useful where contracts are enforceable, customer quality is sound and pricing is understood. The IFC has highlighted investments in trade receivables and digital invoices as mechanisms for extending financing to smaller firms \[2\]. Such products can improve liquidity, but fees, recourse rights and concentration risks need to be assessed. Selling an invoice does not repair permanently unprofitable work. If the margin is wrong, faster payment merely accelerates the discovery.

## Distinguish a liquidity problem from a profitability problem

A company can have temporary liquidity stress because a solvent customer pays late. It can also lose money on every unit sold. These problems need different treatments. The first may justify disciplined receivables financing or renegotiated payment terms. The second requires repricing, cost reduction, redesign or discontinuation. Borrowing to finance persistent negative margins postpones the question and increases the eventual bill.

Calculate gross profit by customer and contract before debating expansion. Reconcile the gross margin to actual fulfilment costs. Review credit limits and concentration, then assess how many weeks of payroll and fixed overheads can be paid from available liquidity without counting uncertain receipts. Make responsibility explicit: sales owns credit-quality information, operations owns delivery acceptance, finance owns the cash forecast, and the executive team owns difficult trade-offs.

Cash discipline is also cultural. Do not reward executives for bringing revenue forward while pushing unavoidable costs into the next reporting period. Build incentives around cash realised, customer satisfaction and sustainable margins together.

## Make cash behaviour visible across the company

Sales teams should be rewarded on collectible contribution, not just contracts signed. Project managers should understand the cash effect of scope changes and delayed completion certificates. Procurement should know which commitments lock in cash before associated revenues arrive. Finance should publish a short weekly cash meeting record: opening balance, confirmed receipts, priority payments, disputes, covenant risks and decisions.

For a practical reset, review the ten largest receivables and ten largest payables every Monday. Assign owners and dated next actions. Identify customers with recurring document rejections or ambiguous approval chains. Renegotiate work that requires the business to carry excessive funding for the customer. Where payment practices deteriorate, elevate the matter commercially before it becomes a legal argument.

Create three triggers: a customer payment overdue beyond an agreed threshold, forecast minimum cash below the board-approved buffer and gross margin below the price floor. Specify the escalation and authority for each trigger. Without consequences, dashboards become an attractive way to watch a problem grow.

A disciplined cash culture also knows when not to chase revenue. Rejecting a large contract with weak margin and punishing terms can protect more enterprise value than winning it. The corporate celebration for “record sales” should perhaps include a brief check of the bank statement before the cake is ordered.

## Boardroom takeaway

Publish cash conversion, aged receivables, overdue supplier accounts and thirteen-week liquidity alongside revenue and profit. A business is not financially healthy because a spreadsheet says next month will be excellent.
