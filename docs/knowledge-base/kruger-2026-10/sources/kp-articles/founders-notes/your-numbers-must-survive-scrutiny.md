# Your Numbers Must Survive Scrutiny

*If the numbers cannot be defended, the deal cannot be trusted.*

FOUNDER VOICE | KRUGER PHIRI | CAPITAL & FUNDING | 18 OCTOBER 2026  

## 1 \| Arithmetic is not credibility

A spreadsheet can multiply perfectly while telling a completely unreliable story. This is the first lesson every founder should learn before walking into a credit committee or investor meeting. The formulas may all work. The problem is that the projected market share has no source, the collection cycle contradicts customer contracts, a cost line has disappeared between model versions and last month's actuals have been replaced by a conveniently optimistic estimate. Excel is innocent. Its operator may have a few questions to answer.

Numbers earn trust when they are traceable, consistent, current and connected to operating reality. If the financial forecast predicts cash collections on day thirty while major customers routinely pay on day ninety, the model must explain what will change. If capex falls by 12% between presentations, show the revised quotations or scope. If the project IRR rises while construction cost increases and revenue stays flat, check the formulas before celebrating a financial miracle.

The OECD's 2023 corporate-governance principles emphasise timely and accurate material disclosure, including performance, financial position and foreseeable risks. The standard is a governance benchmark, not a direct legal rule for every private venture, but it captures what sensible counterparties demand. \[1\] The investor's question is not whether the founder believes. It is whether a sceptical third party can reconstruct the financial argument.

Your forecast is not a prophecy. It is a collection of assumptions that should be exposed to challenge. The purpose of scrutiny is to improve decisions, not humiliate management. Founders who treat every question as a personal attack usually reveal more about the model's weakness than any adverse scenario ever could.

## 2 \| One version of the financial truth

Build a controlled model with clearly identified input cells, calculation sheets, outputs and a change log. State the valuation date, reporting currency, units and treatment of inflation. Define whether figures are nominal or real. Identify tax assumptions and accounting policies. Reconcile historical results to signed accounts or clearly label unaudited management data. Then lock the approved reference version so meetings cannot become competitions between five contradictory attachments.

## 3 \| Make the economics explain themselves

Start with an operating bridge. Revenue equals units multiplied by realised price, adjusted for discounts or other relevant drivers. Gross contribution equals revenue less relevant direct costs. Operating cash flow reflects actual timing and applicable movements in working capital. Capital expenditure and financing flows then determine liquidity. The chain should be visible. A model that skips from "African market opportunity" to "returns above target" has left the room before the interesting questions begin.

For services, scrutinise billable days, utilisation, fees actually collected, subcontractor costs, churn and collection periods. For retail, inspect volumes, returns, shrinkage, stock days and gross margin. For infrastructure, examine activation dates, availability, tariffs, occupancy, maintenance, construction drawdowns and debt service. In each case, connect assumptions to contracts, history or qualified benchmarks. Do not call an assumption a fact because it has been formatted in blue.

## 4 \| Test the cash story separately

Profitability and liquidity can diverge. IAS 7 distinguishes operating, investing and financing cash flows, a useful framework for understanding the source and use of cash. \[2\] A business funded by additional debt may report a positive closing cash balance while ordinary operations consume cash. That may be acceptable during a planned build phase. It is not acceptable to conceal the dependency while describing the business as self-financing.

Use a worked example. A company records R10 million annual revenue at a 30% gross margin. That suggests R3 million gross profit before overhead, financing and tax. If receivables rise substantially and inventory expands, operating cash can still be negative. The calculation is illustrative, not an actual corporate result. The lesson is that income statements alone do not tell funders when money will arrive or which obligations are already committed.

Reconcile opening cash plus receipts minus payments to closing cash. Tie debt balances to drawdowns, repayments and interest. Tie project cost to procurement schedules and approved contingencies. Check balance-sheet movements and tax treatment. Missing links create apparently small errors that compound across forecast years. The expensive errors are often found in the assumptions, not in addition and subtraction.

## 5 \| Stress the variables that can hurt you

Every material model needs a downside analysis that is severe enough to reveal vulnerabilities but grounded enough to inform decisions. Test volume, price, cost escalation, delays, exchange rates, collection periods and interest rates where relevant. Show which variable breaks debt service, liquidity or investor return. Do not label the most optimistic forecast "base case" and call everything else an unfortunate surprise.

Be cautious with precision. An IRR reported to two decimal places may be a valid output of the model, yet it can imply a certainty the assumptions do not deserve. Report the calculated figure alongside ranges and the conditions that move it. Explain whether the IRR is project-level, equity-level, nominal, real, pre-tax or post-tax. Otherwise, two parties can debate an apparently identical percentage while measuring different things.

## 6 \| The adversarial review nobody enjoys

Before releasing numbers, appoint a reviewer who did not build the model. Ask that person to trace every headline claim to its underlying calculation and evidence. Inspect the sign and direction of formula links. Reconcile totals between the financial model, executive summary and term sheet. Check whether contingencies have been counted twice or not at all. Search for hard-coded cells hidden in forecast periods. Validate that chart axes and denominators are not misleading.

Then have commercial, technical and legal advisers challenge their respective assumptions. A financial analyst cannot certify land ownership. An engineer cannot guarantee customer credit. A lawyer cannot determine achievable plant utilisation simply from a clause in a supply agreement. The model is credible when different disciplines agree on the underlying transaction.

A founder should be prepared to say "we do not yet know" and show the plan to find out. That is more bankable than an invented answer. Audiences can forgive uncertainty. They struggle to forgive discovering that certainty was manufactured.

## 7 \| Your twelve-question model examination

Before the next board or investor meeting, answer these questions in a written checklist. What historical data has been reconciled? Which revenue assumptions are contracted? Which remain management estimates? Who approved the cost baseline? What is the working-capital logic? Which debt covenants matter? Where is the minimum-cash month? How does a delayed start change receipts and interest? Which taxes are included? How is sponsor funding evidenced? Can a reviewer reproduce the headline return? Which assumptions would cause management to stop or redesign the project?

Give each material assumption an owner, a source, a last-reviewed date and a confidence grade. This does not make forecasting perfect. It makes weaknesses visible and prevents the business from repeatedly debating the same unknowns as though they were new. The source register should be as important as the formula library.

## 8 \| Final provocation

Numbers should survive the absence of the person who prepared them. They should withstand challenge by a director, an accountant, a commercial partner and someone who has no reason to believe the founder's projections. When they fail a reasonable question, correct them. Do not retaliate with louder adjectives.

A defensible model is not the model with the highest return. It is the one whose assumptions, mechanisms, risks and limitations can be understood and tested. Investors fund opportunities, but they also judge whether management can distinguish measurement from theatre. If the numbers cannot survive scrutiny, please do not ask the deal to carry them into the future. It will collapse under the weight of the footnotes.
