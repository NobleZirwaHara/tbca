# Infrastructure Investment: Building Kenya's Future

*Kenya needs infrastructure that delivers services and survives scrutiny, not simply projects that reach a groundbreaking ceremony.*

By The Business Clinic.Africa | 1 October 2026 | Field note  

## The financing gap is not the only gap

Infrastructure connects economic activity: reliable power keeps factories operating, transport links markets, and water and digital systems determine whether new businesses can scale. The challenge is that an attractive project can fail to deliver value when demand assumptions, land access, environmental requirements, procurement terms, engineering or operations are not mature. A financing announcement is not the same as a constructed asset; a constructed asset is not yet the same as a useful service.

Kenya’s Public Private Partnerships Directorate publishes project pipeline reports, including a status report dated 30 September 2026 and an investment portfolio update in August 2026 \[1\]. These documents matter because PPP projects exist at different stages: identification, feasibility, procurement, financial close, construction and operations. Treating every item in a pipeline as committed or completed investment would materially mislead readers.

The directorate’s fourth annual PPP report for FY2024/25 reported cumulative private capital mobilised under PPPs of KES 145 billion, including KES 17.7 billion mobilised in that financial year \[2\]. These are programme reporting figures, not a claim that all capital has been converted into finished assets or that projects had equal risk. The distinction between financing, construction progress and realised benefits is central to credible infrastructure commentary.

## Prioritise useful capacity over impressive announcements

Start with service outcomes. A road project should specify journey time, safety, maintenance, accessibility and expected freight demand. A power project should specify dependable energy delivered and the customers able to pay, not only installed capacity. Water infrastructure should measure safe water reaching households and losses reduced, not simply kilometres of pipe laid. For a public facility, define affordability and operating budgets before approving construction.

The World Bank’s Private Participation in Infrastructure database reports US\$100.7 billion of commitments in low- and middle-income countries in 2024, up 16% from 2023 \[3\]. This is a global figure, not a Kenyan total. Its relevance is that private participation is material but heterogeneous. Different projects need different revenue models, public guarantees, regulatory structures, risk allocation and financing tenors. PPP is a procurement and financing approach, not a synonym for free infrastructure.

A Kenyan developer should test the full operating case before seeking funding: who uses the service, who pays, in which currency, at what tariff, with what collection risk and for how long? A contract with a strong counterparty can be valuable, but it does not remove construction risk, changes in demand or exposure to law and policy. This is why the lender’s checklist often looks less exciting than the ministerial launch brochure.

## Project preparation is an investment in avoided surprises

A credible preparation pack contains demand analysis, alternatives assessment, land and wayleave evidence, permits, environmental and social impact work, concept engineering, cost estimates, a quantified risk register and a realistic construction schedule. Independent technical review is useful when assumptions could move the budget materially. Cost estimates should state their base date, currency, taxes, contingency and inflation treatment. “Roughly affordable” is not a financial model.

The PPP Directorate’s published feasibility guidance and frameworks offer context for Kenya-specific project development \[4\]. A particular project may also be governed by procurement, sector, environment, land and finance rules. These require qualified local review before a transaction is launched. The point is not to make process an excuse for paralysis. It is to avoid reaching financial close with unresolved problems that are much more expensive to repair during construction.

Apply stage gates. No procurement without a validated service need; no financing process without risk allocation and land strategy; no notice to proceed without critical permits and executable contracts; no declaration of success without commissioning and performance tests. Track the gap between promised and delivered service after operations begin. Opening-day photographs are not a maintenance plan.

## A dashboard that would change decisions

Give each priority project seven fields: service objective, readiness stage, capital required, funding committed, critical permits, delivery forecast and benefits evidence. Add the top unresolved risk and its accountable owner. Distinguish actual spending from budget, physical progress from invoicing and future economic benefits from benefits already measured. That distinction would improve discussion in an investment committee, a county office or a company board.

Prioritise a portfolio using urgency, economic benefit, social inclusion, readiness and fiscal exposure. Do not average away a fatal barrier: if the legal right to the site is absent, the project may not yet be ready regardless of its score on other criteria. Where public finance is involved, assess contingent liabilities and long-term affordability as seriously as up-front construction cost.

Kenya’s infrastructure opportunity will be won by teams that finance projects which are ready, procure them transparently, manage construction competently and operate them reliably. The ambition should be visible in the service delivered, not only in the number of names on the groundbreaking programme.

## What an investment committee should stress-test

Separate four numbers that project summaries regularly mix together: initial capital cost, total financing requirement including interest and contingencies, lifecycle operating and maintenance expenditure, and the economic value of service delivered. A road that is cheap to build but too expensive to maintain is not necessarily good value. A water facility with an uncertain operating budget can become a stranded public asset even when construction finishes on schedule. Report these costs in consistent price years and currencies.

Take an illustrative project with construction expenditure of KES 8 billion and a risk contingency of KES 800 million. The contingency is 10% of the stated base construction cost, not proof that ten percent covers every risk. If construction cost rises by 15%, the extra KES 1.2 billion exceeds the KES 800 million buffer by KES 400 million. That gap must be funded, redesigned away or allocated contractually. State whether the estimate includes financing charges, taxes, land costs and inflation before comparing alternatives.

For projects whose revenue depends on users paying a tariff, test volumes, affordability, collection performance and currency exposure under adverse conditions. For availability-payment PPPs, evaluate the public body’s ability to sustain payments over the concession, including contingent liabilities. Commercial lenders and public authorities may value different outcomes, but they must agree on the transaction’s underlying facts. Financial close should not depend on unresolved demand evidence or permits.

During delivery, independently verify earned milestones and changes to scope. During operations, publish indicators for reliability, capacity, safety, maintenance and user complaints. Compare benefits with the original investment case, not just the latest budget. That final comparison is what converts a construction programme into an institutional learning system. Infrastructure creates development when the service works year after year, including on the unphotographed Tuesdays.

## Boardroom takeaway

**Require a named owner, a credible baseline, a measurable improvement and a decision date. Revisit the evidence before committing the next tranche of time, money or reputation.**
