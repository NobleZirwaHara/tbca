# Doing Business in Nigeria: What the Data Actually Shows.

*Nigeria offers genuine commercial scale, but success depends on unit economics, currency resilience and regulatory diligence.*

By The Business Clinic.Africa | 02 October 2026 | Pan-African Business Analysis  

## Large market, difficult arithmetic

Nigeria is an important African economy with a large consumer and business market. Neither statement is a go-to-market strategy. A population chart does not show how many customers can afford your product, reach your distribution point or pay on time. Investors must decide whether they are underwriting opportunity or merely admiring scale.

The IMF’s June 2026 Article IV assessment estimated Nigeria’s real economic growth at 4.0% in 2025 and noted that inflation was 15.4% year on year in March 2026 \[1\]. It reported that national-poverty-line poverty reached 63%, while around 27 million Nigerians were estimated to have faced food insecurity in autumn 2025. These measures come from different reference periods and serve different analytical purposes. Growth indicates aggregate activity; it does not establish the purchasing power of a target segment.

The same IMF assessment recorded gross official reserves of approximately US\$46 billion at end-2025, up from around US\$40 billion at end-2024, while noting differences between the Central Bank’s reserve definition and the IMF’s methodology \[1\]. Headline reserves are not a promise that an individual importer will obtain foreign currency on the day its suppliers demand payment.

Nigeria should therefore be assessed through three lenses: **customer economics, operating friction and institutional risk.** Customer economics require addressable demand at a realistic price. Operating friction includes electricity, logistics, payments and fulfilment. Institutional risk includes taxes, permits, enforcement, dispute resolution and changes in policy. The opportunity is real, but so is the invoice for entering badly.

## What a defensible market entry looks like

The World Bank’s Business Ready framework evaluates regulatory quality, public services and how effectively those rules and services operate in practice \[2\]. This is the right analytical distinction. A licence that can legally be issued in ten days may still require operational verification of the actual process, jurisdiction and costs. Firms need to map the rule, test the process and budget for variance.

Segment Nigeria by product and channel rather than announcing a national launch. Lagos consumer demand, an industrial procurement customer in Ogun and an agricultural distribution route in the north present different economics and operational risks. Select two or three reachable customer cohorts and test willingness to pay through signed orders, deposits or controlled pilots. Avoid treating social-media engagement as bankable demand.

A foreign entrant should build a local legal and tax map before pricing. Confirm entity establishment, beneficial ownership obligations, sector licensing, import rules, VAT and payroll requirements with competent Nigerian advisers. Work through customs classification and the real landed cost, including freight, duties, delays and inland distribution. Model naira exposure and cash conversion explicitly. Hedging or local sourcing may reduce certain risks, but neither can eliminate uncertainty.

A practical case: a distributor sells imported equipment on sixty-day customer terms while its overseas supplier expects payment on shipment. If the exchange rate weakens before collection, accounting revenue may look healthy while the cash margin disappears. The solution may be customer deposits, shorter credit terms, a local-currency cost base or contract mechanisms where legally and commercially possible. The correct answer depends on the transaction; the point is to discover the exposure before the first container sails.

## Separate country risk from transaction risk

A national economic indicator is one layer of the investment case. A transaction requires a second layer: specific customers, contracts, banks, regulators, transport corridors and enforceable remedies. A company can succeed in a difficult macroeconomic setting if it controls its segment well. A company can also fail in a growing economy if it sells below cost or cannot collect.

Prepare a counterparty map covering the beneficial ownership of commercial partners, payment history, operational capacity, conflicts and sanctions exposure where applicable. Review bank arrangements and settlement processes in the intended currency, with legal advice where required. Make sure key contracts allocate taxes, delivery, force majeure, quality claims and dispute resolution to parties that can manage them.

Finally, model three market-entry cases: low sales and slower collections, a base case established by the pilot, and expansion following demonstrated repeat demand. Investment should be released against observed milestones. This approach allows an executive to remain optimistic about Nigeria without requiring the finance director to suspend disbelief.

## An entry plan with decision gates

Days 1–30: identify target customers, interview buyers and verify willingness to pay. Obtain written estimates for licensing, logistics, warehousing, payment processing and professional services. Build a model with product-level gross margin and a separate working-capital schedule. Test how the model responds to inflation, foreign-exchange movements and delayed settlement.

Days 31–60: run a geographically bounded pilot with a local partner whose capabilities and conflicts have been checked. Define service levels, payment triggers, data access and dispute mechanisms in writing. Evaluate the partner against actual deliveries, not introductions to important people. Nigerian markets have enough complexity without paying a premium for impressive business cards.

Days 61–90: compare the pilot with predetermined criteria: repeat-purchase rate, cost-to-serve, receivable age, regulatory compliance, customer complaints and contribution margin after fulfilment. Expand only if those results are sustainable. If they are not, revise the operating model before deploying additional capital.

Two cautions matter. First, macroeconomic reforms can improve conditions while households remain under pressure. The IMF expressly warned that reform gains had not eliminated hardship \[1\]. Second, national indicators do not substitute for current state, city and sector diligence. No national report can validate a specific lease, counterparty, licence or tax position.

## Boardroom takeaway

Approve a Nigerian entry on verified local unit economics, tested compliance and a funded cash cycle. Do not approve it because “Africa’s largest opportunity” looks wonderful on slide three. Geography is context. Execution creates the return.
