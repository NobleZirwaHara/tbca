# The 7 Signals Investors Look For (But Rarely Say).

*The meeting may focus on your forecast, but the investment decision is often determined by the evidence around it.*

By The Business Clinic.Africa | 08 October 2026 | Pan-African Business Analysis  

## The deck is a conversation starter, not the asset

Founders often believe investors judge ideas according to presentation quality. Presentations matter, but capital providers ultimately assess whether a venture can produce value while controlling downside risk. The IFC estimated a US\$5.7 trillion global MSME financing gap in its April 2025 communication \[1\]. That is evidence of financing friction, not evidence that investors have abandoned sensible underwriting.

Signal one is intellectual honesty. Strong founders know what they have tested, what remains assumed and what would disprove the investment thesis. They distinguish signed orders from a pipeline, recurring revenue from one-off sales and net income from operating cash. The person who says “we have no competition” has usually announced that the market analysis is unfinished.

Signal two is customer proof. Show repeat purchases, collection records, unit-level economics and reasons buyers select the offer. For pre-revenue infrastructure or research ventures, use appropriate proof instead: binding offtake, credible feasibility, pilots, technical validation or public procurement approvals. Evidence changes by sector; marketing enthusiasm does not qualify as evidence in any sector.

Signal three is capital discipline. Investors want a clear connection between the amount requested, the work it funds and the milestone it should create. An expense list is not a funding strategy. A phased proposal that identifies how risks decline at each gate is easier to interrogate and sometimes easier to finance. The firm may still be rejected because of mandate or pricing, but at least the investment case can be understood.

Four quieter signals. Signal four is governance quality. Clean ownership records, conflict disclosures, board processes and transparent related-party dealings indicate whether decisions are being made for the enterprise rather than the convenience of insiders.

Signal five is operating resilience. Investors ask whether customers, systems and delivery depend on one indispensable founder. A business where every approval sits with the chief executive is not lean; it is a queue with a title.

Signal six is the ability to learn. A team that revises pricing after pilot evidence is often more credible than one that protects an initial forecast from all contact with reality. The Project Management Institute’s 2024 survey reported an average project performance rate of 73.8% across respondents, measuring the self-reported share of completed projects meeting business goals \[2\]. It is not an investor return benchmark. Its lesson is that delivery performance can be measured and improved, rather than presumed from confidence.

Signal seven is deal realism. A venture should understand dilution, covenants, repayment schedules, security, governance rights and the cost of missing targets. The same R20 million may create very different risk depending on whether it comes as patient equity or amortising debt. A low nominal interest rate is not automatically cheap if principal is due before the business can generate cash.

Serious due diligence also examines concentration. If one customer drives most revenue, it will test renewal risk and payment behaviour. If one supplier controls critical input, it will assess alternatives. If growth demands the founder personally sell every contract, it will question scalability. None of these questions requires the investor to dislike the business. They require the investor to understand what could go wrong.

## What the evidence should look like at each stage

In a pre-revenue venture, customer validation may consist of controlled pilots, technical approvals and independently tested willingness to pay. In an established service company, the same question can be answered with cohorts, renewal history and customer concentration analysis. In a long-life project, lenders may require enforceable offtake terms and completion support. The signal is constant; the relevant evidence is not.

Evaluate the management team with a capability matrix. Name the executives who own sales, finance, operations and compliance; identify vacancies and succession risks. Test forecasting discipline by comparing prior plans with actual results. Investors do not require perfect forecasts, but they value credible explanations of variance and a process that improves the next estimate.

Agree the investor’s conditions early: minimum ticket, sector exclusions, impact mandates, risk limits and investment horizon. This saves founders from spending months polishing applications for capital that was never eligible for the deal. It also reveals when the business needs debt restructuring, a smaller pilot or partner capital before approaching new equity.

## Turn the seven signals into a data room

Create seven folders, one per signal. Put customer evidence in the customer folder, not buried in a fifty-slide appendix. Provide bank reconciliations and the assumptions behind the cash model. Include a risk register with owners and mitigation budgets. Make legal documents current. List contracts by status: executed, conditional, draft or discussion. This distinction protects both sides.

Give investors a one-page answer to five core questions: What creates demand? Why will this team win? What generates cash? What could destroy the thesis? Which proof point will the requested capital buy? Then prepare a separate downside case. A model that survives only when every customer pays early, every permit arrives on time and every exchange rate cooperates is less a forecast than a birthday wish.

Practise a difficult investment committee. Ask one reviewer to attack market demand, one to challenge working capital and a third to test governance. Record unresolved issues and agree which must be closed before funds move. Where confidentiality or commercial sensitivity matters, use an appropriately controlled data-room process rather than circulating everything by email.

Finally, know the right investor. An institution mandated to provide short-term trade facilities may never invest in ten-year infrastructure risk. Rejection can reflect investment policy, transaction size or portfolio concentration rather than the quality of a venture. The job is to find aligned capital, not to collect polite rejections from every institution on the continent.

## Boardroom takeaway

Score each of the seven signals red, amber or green before fundraising. The most valuable work may be turning two red signals green, not scheduling twenty more pitches.
