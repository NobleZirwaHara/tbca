 1 Purpose and implementation decisions
 TBCA should keep its Laravel customer experience and connect it to Zoho One as the operating
 backbone. Configure a reliable customer record, controlled payment and delivery workflows, and a
 single client dashboard before expanding automation. Use rules and APIs for predictable work, AI
 agents for language and evidence-based assistance, and RPA for approved tasks that have no usable
 API.

 This guide is written for the TBCA business owner, app administrator, customer service manager and
 implementation partner. It contains a ranked shortlist of 30 Zoho applications, configuration
 instructions, data and integration specifications, automation recipes, an AI and RPA implementation
 course, reusable prompts, and launch tests. Configuration values are proposed TBCA defaults, not
 settings read from the private Zoho tenant. Research date: 11 October 2026.

 The public TBCA homepage, portal landing page, due-diligence page, virtual-deal-room page and AIIM
 page were inspected. Authenticated customer workflows, source code, payment processing and the
 private Zoho tenant were not inspected. The supplied Zoho workspace redirected to sign-in. Verify the
 tenant edition, region, licences, app versions and current menu names before following production
 steps. No TBCA or Zoho settings were changed while preparing this guide.

 Security, certification, institutional partnerships, country coverage and intelligence quality shown on
 the website are marketing statements, not verified implementation evidence. Before reproducing
 them in automated messages, attach the current supporting record and owner approval. Zoho
 subscription ownership alone does not establish TBCA certification or legal compliance.

 The ideal customer path
 1. Discover: the visitor chooses language and currency, sees a clear service description, scope,
 indicative timetable and the action to take.

 2. Diagnose: the visitor completes a short diagnostic, sees an explanation of the result, and can
 request a human discovery call. Keep diagnosis separate from regulated financial or legal advice.

 3. Register: the customer verifies their email, creates or joins a business account, and sees only that
 business’s records. Customer identity is not a staff Zoho licence.

 4. Select and agree: show the approved product version, deliverables, exclusions, tax treatment,
 payment schedule, cancellation rules and authorised agreement.

 5. Pay: use a supported hosted gateway or invoice payment link. Confirm payment on the server
 before granting paid access. An on-screen success page alone is insufficient.

 6. Onboard: show a short checklist, collect necessary evidence, confirm consent and mandate, and
 offer a booking link without asking for the same details again.

 7. Collaborate: display milestones, due dates, customer actions and an accountable TBCA owner.
 Notify only when the customer needs to act or a meaningful status changes.

 8. Review and deliver: a named expert approves advisory deliverables. The customer receives a portal
 notice and downloads the approved version under the correct permissions.

 9. Support and improve: retain a ticket history, request a short satisfaction response, resolve problems
 and suggest a suitable next service only under valid marketing preferences.

 3 Prepare the organisation before adding workflows
 Complete this section before configuring the 30 applications. Importance rank is not installation order:
 Directory and Vault are foundations and should be prepared on day one. Use a pilot group and dummy
 customers until launch tests pass.

 1. Name an executive sponsor with authority over scope, an implementation lead, a Zoho
 administrator, a Laravel integration developer, a finance owner, a privacy owner and a
 customer-experience owner. One person may hold several roles, but high-risk approvals require an
 independent authorised reviewer.

 2. Create a decision register. Record each decision, date, owner and evidence: legal entity, registered
 details, trading address, domain ownership, data centre, base currency, tax registrations, supported
 countries and service hours. Do not invent registration or tax numbers.

 3. Sign into the supplied TBCA Zoho One account. Confirm the organisation name and ID, existing
 organisations inside Finance apps, data centre and employee licensing model. Record app editions and
 limits. Do not create a second Books organisation accidentally.

 4. In the One administration area, locate App Management and Applications. Some versions call the
 entry Admin Panel, Directory Settings or Directory. Choose Add Application and the Zoho app
 catalogue, select each needed app, and assign only its pilot users. If an app is missing, check
 permissions and regional entitlement with Zoho support.

 5. Create staff groups: Administration, Sales, Delivery, Support, Finance, Research, Compliance and
 Automation Operations. Give app access by responsibility. Keep customer users outside staff groups.

 6. For every app, confirm its organisation and timezone. Proposed initial defaults are
 Africa/Johannesburg and ZAR for TBCA’s South African entity. Store event times in UTC and display
 each customer’s local timezone. Confirm financial year and business hours with management.

 7. Record domains to use. Suggested names are app.thebusinessclinic.africa,
 help.thebusinessclinic.africa, book.thebusinessclinic.africa and learn.thebusinessclinic.africa. These
 are proposals, not existing verified hosts. An administrator must verify ownership and add the
 app-specific DNS records. Check that HTTPS works before inviting customers.

 8. Set up separate development and test environments where the product permits. Where no full
 sandbox exists, use isolated pilot records, restricted templates and recipients, and disabled payment
 and sending actions. Never test a live broadcast on the customer list.

 9. Apply MFA to staff, use dedicated limited integration identities where supported, and establish
 emergency recovery with two trusted administrators. Test recovery and offboarding. Do not let agents
 use the organisation owner’s unrestricted credentials.

 10. Collect approved brand assets, email signatures, privacy text, service terms, consent wording,
 diagnostic questions, service catalogue, proposal template, report template and DD review checklist.
 Record the owner and approved version of each.

 11. Create two dummy customer businesses with separate owners and staff, plus a dummy partner
 and investor. Keep all test documents fictitious. These accounts will prove customer isolation across
 every feature.

 12. For each new setting, record old value, new value, reason, approver, screenshot or export, test
 result and rollback procedure. Make one controlled change at a time so errors can be traced.

 - DD case: Requested > Purpose and mandate verified > Consent recorded > Payment verified >
 Evidence complete > Checks running > Expert review > Report approved > Delivered > Monitoring or
 Closed. Block missing prerequisites.

 - Deal room access request: Requested > Identity checked > NDA complete > Disclosure approved >
 Active > Expired or Revoked. No single signed NDA grants access to every file.

 - Subscription: Pending > Active > Past due > Grace period > Suspended > Cancelled or Expired. Map
 actual Billing states to these customer-friendly states; do not assume the names match.

 Practical rules
 - Finance integrations may be two-way natively, but the business must still nominate where staff edit
 each field. Do not try to turn a two-way integration into a one-way one by simply labelling it so.

 - Resolve duplicates through an owner-reviewed merge. Deduplicate by stable IDs; use normalised
 email only as a secondary clue. Never merge two businesses just because they share a contact.

 - Customer-facing requests must resolve business membership, permission and entitlement on the
 server before looking up Zoho data. A hidden field or browser filter is not an access control.

 - Approve retention schedules by data category and jurisdiction. Financial records, DD evidence,
 marketing data and chat logs need separate policies; legal hold may override deletion.

 6 How to follow the application configuration chapters
 Each chapter states its owner, prerequisites, practical entry point, steps, automation opportunity and
 acceptance tests. Exact click-by-click labels are verified only where current documentation supplies
 them; paths described more generally require an administrator to match the equivalent settings in the
 tenant. Do not invent a missing feature or switch on a paid upgrade to make a step appear.

 1. Complete the foundation and common-record sections first. Open the app inside the correct TBCA
 organisation and confirm the environment.

 2. Locate the settings listed in the chapter. If the label differs, use the app’s setup search or the linked
 official help; verify your edition/version. If the feature is absent, record it as a dependency and use the
 stated fallback.

 3. Follow the numbered steps with dummy data, then run every acceptance test. Save evidence in the
 implementation register.

 4. Record automation owner, service identity, app limits, failure route and rollback. A connected app is
 not a tested customer journey.

 5. Only then replace dummy records with approved production configuration and enable the required
 live workflows. This guide defines the configuration; tenant-specific implementation requires the
 administrator and integration developer.

 7.1 Configure Zoho CRM
 Keeps each TBCA business relationship, prospect, contact and opportunity together; the first
 operational anchor for every sale and engagement.

 Customer experience: Customers repeat fewer details, receive relevant follow-up and have a named
 account owner.

   Responsible owner         Before you start                        Where to begin


   Sales operations          Approved customer model, product        CRM Setup; look for Customization, Modules and
                             references, staff roles and two dummy   Fields, Security Control and Process Management.
                             businesses.




 Configuration steps
 1. Open Accounts and create a dummy business. Open Contacts and link its owner to that Account.
 Keep Leads for unqualified prospects and Deals for specific opportunities.

 2. In Modules and Fields, edit the Account layout. Add TBCA Business ID, Country, Preferred language,
 Billing contact, Service owner and Customer status. Mark Business ID unique where supported;
 otherwise enforce uniqueness in the integration.

 3. Add TBCA Person ID to Contacts and separate marketing preference fields. Store consent evidence
 in the consent ledger, with a reference in CRM.

 4. Edit Deals. Add Order ID, Engagement ID, SKU, Quote version, Currency, Payment verification state,
 Contract ID and Project ID. Do not store screening evidence in sales notes.

 5. Configure the proposed sales stages from section 4. Create a service-opportunity pipeline suitable
 for Sprints and a separately governed route for complex mandates if the edition supports it.

 6. Create products for the pilot Sprints using approved SKU references. Keep displayed prices aligned
 to the owning catalogue; tax and contractual totals come from the finance process.

 7. Create roles and profiles for Sales, Account manager and Read-only reviewer. Make sensitive
 modules private and grant only the required sharing. Restrict bulk export to authorised staff.

 8. Set duplicate checks for stable external IDs. Test lead conversion so the resulting Account and
 Contact retain their TBCA IDs without duplicating an existing business.

 9. Go to Process Management and Blueprint. Select Deals and the Stage field. Build transitions for
 qualification, proposal and contract approval; require scope version and the authorised owner at the
 relevant transitions.

 10. Create a workflow for new web leads: assign an owner and create a follow-up task. Acknowledge
 once through the nominated sending system; disable duplicate native acknowledgements.

 11. Connect Desk, Projects and Finance only after checking existing integrations. Map external IDs

 Reliable event handling step by step
 1. Receive an event over HTTPS and authenticate the sender. For TBCA-controlled senders, a
 developer can use HMAC and replay protection. Use the gateway’s documented verification method
 for its own events.

 2. Do not assume every Zoho native webhook supplies a cryptographic body signature. Use supported
 header authentication or a controlled relay as appropriate, and verify the source record with the
 relevant API before sensitive state changes.

 3. Validate the payload and source organisation. Reject unknown event types and inconsistent
 business/order references. Keep sensitive fields out of routine logs.

 4. Store the event durably before acknowledging receipt. Add a database uniqueness constraint to the
 event ID and source; a simple search-then-create check can race.

 5. Queue a job and lock the affected order or engagement while applying the state change. Ignore an
 older status version when a newer accepted state exists.

 6. Write a durable step ledger with planned action, idempotency key, state and returned external ID.
 Before retrying a timed-out create, search or verify whether it already succeeded.

 7. Retry temporary network errors and rate limits with a bounded delay and jitter. Honour documented
 retry headers. Authentication and validation failures need correction, not endless retries.

 8. Where APIs lack a native idempotency key, enforce business-key uniqueness and operation locks in
 the integration; reconcile the target record before repeating a side effect.

 9. Send each customer message through a notification ledger keyed by event, recipient and template
 version. Store verified delivery attempt and status.

 10. When a step repeatedly fails, place it in an exception queue and alert operations. Show an
 accurate customer state such as Payment received, preparing your workspace.

 11. Reconcile every day: payments against orders, orders against invoices, paid engagements against
 projects, subscriptions against entitlements and approved reports against delivered files.

 12. Implement pause controls for outbound messages, new financial writes, AI write tools and RPA
 triggers. Stopping a risky automation must not hide existing customer records.

 Database uniqueness, queues and record locks require developer implementation. Zoho Flow alone
 does not create distributed exactly-once transactions. Design for repeated delivery and verified
 recovery. Match Laravel queue configuration to the installed framework version and deployment
 runtime.

 Permission before retrieval for deal rooms and AIIM

 12 Measure progress toward 90 percent automation
 Treat 90% as a target to validate, not a capability guaranteed by Zoho or a reason to bypass review.
 Define it first as the share of eligible routine task instances completed successfully without staff
 intervention. Keep high-risk decisions human-led. A workflow that contains a human final approval is
 assisted automation, not fully autonomous end-to-end completion.

 Use three measures together
 - Routine straight-through rate = eligible routine tasks completed correctly without human
 intervention / all eligible routine tasks attempted.

 - Overall automation share = all tasks completed correctly without human intervention / all task
 instances, including mandatory reviews and exceptions. This prevents an inflated denominator that
 excludes hard work.

 - Human time share = staff minutes spent on all reviews, corrections and exceptions / total
 comparable process effort. Measure baseline labour time and resulting labour time separately; task
 count and time are not interchangeable.

 Daily operator routine
 1. Review paid orders without complete workspaces, failed jobs, pending customer actions and late
 review tasks.

 2. Reconcile payment/order/invoice references and subscription entitlements. Finance reviews
 discrepancies and settlement status.

 3. Check Flow/API/RPA/AI limits, runner health, queue age, token refresh errors and source-refresh
 status.

 4. Review priority tickets, complaints, low ratings and pending authority/consent decisions.

 5. Sample agent answers and unassisted actions for evidence, permission, correctness and customer
 usefulness.

 6. Keep an exception owner and next action on every blocked case; communicate a truthful customer
 update when it affects service.

 Weekly and monthly review
 - Weekly: investigate top failure causes, update approved knowledge, review agent correction rates
 and inspect repeated customer confusion.

 - Monthly: report the defined automation measures alongside satisfaction, delivery timeliness, cost per
 engagement and unresolved exceptions.

 - Monthly: forecast storage, credits, API calls and runner capacity using observed activity, not
 theoretical bundle size.

 - Quarterly or after material change: review access, jurisdiction/source rights, restore tests, autonomy
 levels and public trust claims.

 Incident and rollback steps
 1. Detect and classify: identify impacted customers, records, services and whether money, confidential
 data or permissions are affected.

 2. Contain: pause the specific sender, AI write tool, workflow or RPA job. Revoke exposed tokens or
 access under the authorised recovery process.

 3. Preserve: retain relevant event IDs, versions, timestamps and protected audit evidence. Do not
 overwrite the failing configuration before recording it.

 4. Reconcile: determine which side effects already happened. Do not replay invoices, refunds,
 signature requests or messages blindly.

 5. Restore: revert the relevant tested version or permissions and recover records/documents
 according to app capability. Configuration rollback does not reverse financial or legal transactions.

 6. Communicate: the accountable incident/privacy/customer owner follows the approved internal and
 customer notification process, including legal duties where applicable.

 7. Validate: re-run affected launch tests, reconcile impacted records and obtain the appropriate
 owner’s approval before resuming.

 17 Plain language glossary
   Term                              Meaning for TBCA


   API                               A supported way for one application to read or change another application’s information
                                     without clicking its screen.


   OAuth                             An authorised connection that grants specified access without sharing an ordinary
                                     account password.


   Webhook                           A notification sent to a server when something changes. The receiver must verify the
                                     source and meaning.


   Trigger                           The event or condition that starts a workflow or agent.


   System of record                  The app whose approved value wins for a particular type of information.


   Mapping                           Matching a field or record in one app to the correct field or record in another.


   Idempotency                       Repeated receipt of the same instruction does not create repeated business effects.


   Reconciliation                    Comparing related records to find missing, conflicting or duplicated results.


   Queue                             A durable waiting list of jobs that workers process without making the customer wait on
                                     every step.


   Entitlement                       The features or documents a customer is currently allowed to use because of an approved
                                     purchase or permission.


   RBAC and ABAC                     Access by role and by attributes such as business, deal, disclosure stage and expiry.


   RAG                               Retrieving authorised evidence before an AI writes an answer; it does not automatically
                                     verify the evidence.


   RPA                               A robot following an approved desktop or browser routine when an API cannot perform
                                     the task.


   Agentic AI                        An assistant selecting approved tools and steps toward a bounded goal with controlled
                                     permissions.


   Human in the loop                 A person makes a defined review or approval before a consequential action proceeds.


   Straight-through completion       A task finishes correctly without staff intervention, including recovery and final checks.


   Tamper-evident                    A design that makes an unauthorised change to records detectable; ordinary editable logs
                                     do not guarantee this.


   Synthetic data                    Demonstration or generated data that must not be presented as verified real-world
                                     intelligence.
